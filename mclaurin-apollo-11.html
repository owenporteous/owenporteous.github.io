Overview

As a flight dynamics research intern at McLaurin Aerospace, I extended the company's MATLAB-based 6-DOF launch vehicle simulation. The assignment was to learn the simulation and implement the Apollo 11 Lunar Module descent stage, giving the company its first vehicle backed by real flown data.

My role

Implementing the descent stage started with research into the Apollo mission architecture. Alongside the flight software, I built supporting infrastructure that the simulation needed to fly the vehicle, described under Simulation infrastructure below.

How it works

The main focus was custom flight software for the vehicle, based on the guidance and navigation algorithms used on the real mission. I reconstructed a simplified version that took in accelerometer and altitude radar data, rotated the coordinates into the inertial frame, and applied the Apollo Average-G routine. From there, the software found the guidance target and guidance coordinate system, calculated the guidance trajectory, and produced an acceleration command for the control system.

Simulation infrastructure

01

Throttleable engines

Added throttleable engine capability to the simulation, using an exponential ramp model to capture the lag between a throttle command and the engine's response, which laid the groundwork for a control system to act on guidance output.

02

Spherical tank ullage calculations

Built calculations that locate the height of the propellant and of the ullage space within a spherical tank.

03

Hand of god developer tool

Built a tool that bypasses the control systems to apply an acceleration directly to the vehicle, making it possible to test the simulation without relying on a propulsion system.

Where it landed

When I left, the vehicle could hover in place and had completed a 1-DOF flight test. Work on the reaction control system (RCS), trajectory mapping, and control systems was still unfinished. Even so, I was proud to have understood the guidance and navigation algorithms well enough to rebuild them in a completely different environment.

What I learned

The project was my introduction to rocket science, spanning flight software, simulation development, and the fundamentals of rocket flight dynamics. Along the way, I learned Runge-Kutta methods for numerical integration, the advanced mathematics behind trajectory generation, and a great deal of guidance and navigation. Practice problems from my supervisor also introduced me to PID control for trajectory correction, which I explored further by building a small simulation of my own.
